AIDE: AI Disclosure of Ideation & Execution

A dual-axis self-disclosure rating scale for software engineering and creative works that separates human conceptual authorship (Ideation) from mechanical production (Execution).

1. Select Project Ideation & Execution Levels

Quick Presets:

Axis 1: Ideation & Design (I0–I4)

Who conceived the ideas, algorithms, system architecture, and creative structure?

I0
Unassisted 100% human-originated concepts, algorithms, and design. Zero AI consultation even for background research.
I1
Assisted Core concepts and algorithms are human-originated. AI used only for background domain research or minor edge-case tweaks tested by the author.
I2
Collaborative Human set the vision and partnered with AI to co-brainstorm sub-algorithms, architecture, or outlines.
I3
Directed Human provided a high-level goal; AI proposed the primary architecture, algorithm choices, or structure, curated by the human.
I4
Autonomous AI conceived the project concept, design, and creative direction with minimal human input.

Axis 2: Execution & Implementation (E0–E4)

How was the code, prose, or media mechanically produced? (Click multiple levels if your project has hybrid components.)

E0
Unassisted 100% hand-crafted code, assembly, prose, or assets by the human author with zero AI involvement.
E1
Assisted Human-authored output. AI used only for read-only bug diagnostics, phrase thesaurus, non-generative signal cleanup, or minor inline boilerplate.
E2
Collaborative Shared human-AI implementation, such as human-written core logic paired with AI-generated helper scripts or data charts.
E3
Directed AI wrote the component's code or content strictly from detailed, multi-paragraph human specifications, algorithms, and testing.
E4
Autonomous Generated end-to-end by AI from minimal prompts with little or no human specification or code review ("vibe-coded").

2. Component-Level Breakdown (Optional for Hybrid Projects)

Define individual project deliverables so readers immediately see which parts are hand-crafted (such as 8086 assembly) and which parts used AI tooling.

Why AIDE Separates Ideation from Execution

      

        Single-axis AI rating systems (such as REAL Rating, GAIT, and linear SPDX tags) conflate conceptual authorship with mechanical labor. Worse, linear scales typically treat AI brainstorming as a low level of AI usage while penalizing projects where a human author invented 100% of the algorithms and specifications but used an LLM to generate a companion utility in a secondary programming language.
      

      

        AIDE (AI Disclosure of Ideation & Execution) solves this by evaluating works across two orthogonal 0-to-4 axes using five symmetrical level titles: 0 (Unassisted), 1 (Assisted), 2 (Collaborative), 3 (Directed), and 4 (Autonomous).

            I0
            Unassisted
            All concepts, algorithms, architecture, and creative designs are 100% human-originated, with zero AI consultation even for background research.
            Designing a novel compression algorithm or hardware routine solely from personal knowledge and paper datasheets; conceiving a story or musical composition independently.
          

          

            I1
            Assisted
            All core concepts, architecture, and primary algorithms are human-originated. AI was used only for background research (explaining domain math/theory, summarizing references) or as a sounding board that suggested minor optimizations or edge-case tweaks which the human tested and selectively adopted.
            Inventing a novel 8-bit PCM lossy compression algorithm, using an LLM to review DSP math, and testing minor edge-case refinements suggested by the model.
          

          

            I2
            Collaborative
            The human established the overarching vision and goals, and actively partnered with AI to brainstorm sub-algorithms, solve structural design problems, or develop outlines together.
            Co-designing subsystem architectures or narrative plot structures through iterative back-and-forth brainstorming sessions with an LLM.
          

          

            I3
            Directed
            The human provided a broad problem statement, theme, or high-level prompt, and AI generated the primary architecture, algorithm choices, or creative structure, which the human then curated and refined.
            Prompting an AI with desired input/output goals and allowing the AI to select the underlying algorithms and architecture, which the human reviews and approves.
          

          

            I4
            Autonomous
            AI conceived the project concept, design, and creative direction with minimal or no human conceptual input.
            Prompting an autonomous agent to invent and design a utility or creative work from a single open-ended request.

            Code
            Title
            Definition
            Software & Creative Examples
          

        
        
          

            E0
            Unassisted
            Every line of code, word of prose, or creative asset was manually authored by the human with zero AI involvement.
            Hand-writing an 8086 assembly decompressor instruction-by-instruction; typing an essay on a mechanical keyboard without AI tools.
          

          

            E1
            Assisted
            The delivered work is human-authored. AI/ML tools were used only for read-only diagnostics (bug-finding, code review), reference lookups (phrase thesaurus), non-generative signal cleanup (audio denoising, speech-to-text transcription), or minor inline autocomplete/boilerplate.
            Writing C or assembly code manually and using an LLM to check for off-by-one bugs; using neural audio filters to clean reverb from field recordings.
          

          

            E2
            Collaborative
            Human and AI each produced substantial portions of the delivered work, such as a human writing the core engine or prose while AI generated supporting utilities, build scripts, or data charts/diagrams.
            Hand-coding a core runtime engine while using an LLM to generate test harnesses, CLI parsers, or charts from human-supplied data.
          

          

            E3
            Directed
            Generative AI wrote the vast majority of the component's code or content, operating strictly as an implementation engine under human direction, detailed multi-paragraph specifications, explicit algorithms, constraints, and human verification/testing.
            Providing an LLM with a complete, human-authored algorithm specification and bit-stream format so it writes a host compressor utility, verified by the author.
          

          

            E4
            Autonomous
            The delivered output was generated end-to-end by AI from minimal prompts with little or no human specification, code review, or manual modification.
            Accepting bulk agent-generated code without reviewing the internal logic ("vibe-coding") or publishing raw synthetic media.
